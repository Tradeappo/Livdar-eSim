## PATH 2 - level-2 and level-3 administrative subdivisions

**Discovery, without the query shape that times out.** `?c wdt:P279* wd:Q13220204 . ?x
wdt:P31 ?c` fails at every endpoint. The shape that answers is the reverse: ask which of a
KNOWN set of class ids are subclasses of the level roots, in VALUES batches. All 2,460 tail
classes were tested that way, and the test had to be run **twice** before it could be
trusted:

- against the three NUMBERED roots (first, second and third-level administrative country
  subdivision): **48 classes, 2,275 readable items** - because Wikidata models a canton of
  France under "administrative division of France" and not under the numbered levels at all;
- adding the generic `administrative territorial entity` root: **1,227 classes** - and the
  set fills with airbases, barracks, observation towers, priories and Japanese castles, which
  reach that root through the subclass graph.

Neither number is the set of containers, so the container test is the one the area contract
already makes. P131 is *located in the administrative territorial entity*, so the share of a
class's items that an admitted page or the containment chain names as its P131 parent is
direct evidence. It separates them cleanly: county of Illinois 102 of 102, county of Missouri
114 of 114, regional unit of Greece 74 of 74; Japanese castle 0 of 1,249, motte-and-bailey
castle 0 of 523, watchtower 0 of 285. The share is recorded per class in
`landmark/_area-routing.json`, and one narrow label test rescues the 162 classes that reach
the administrative root while naming a park, a fort, a barracks, a station or a diocese,
because that is the one direction the generic root leaks.

**The first attempt at this was wrong and the record keeps it.** Leaving the containers in the
landmark directory let the landmark builder page **3,179 administrative areas on two
printable facts and a parent** - 228 powiats, 143 non-metropolitan districts, 94 French
departments, 91 Indian districts, 81 Italian provinces, 70 Texas counties - which is exactly
the generic name-population-area administrative page the area contract exists to refuse,
while the area pass lost those same items to "already paged on another surface" and emitted
**zero rows**. A container is now RESERVED: 102,207 ids the landmark builder refuses outright,
and the area contract admits one on all three of its tests or it gets none.

## The two paths did not close the gap, so the brief's next instruction ran

After both paths the inventory stood at **984,726**, short by 15,274. Three model-discovery
passes followed.

### 3a. A census of every heritage register Wikidata holds an identifier for

Three registers had been chosen by hand from the four countries this project happened to know
about, and the register model converts better than any other here (16 per cent against the
landmark classes' 13). So all **13,977 Wikidata properties** were read and the 299 whose
label names a register of places were counted. **73 hold 500 or more readable items; 70 were
not built.**

Twenty-four more registers were taken, every one kept by a state body or an agency of one:
the US National Register of Historic Places (**71,267 readable items, more than twice
Mérimée**), Bavaria's two, Austria's Denkmalverzeichnis and Heritage Information System,
Flanders, Spain's Bienes de Interés Cultural, the Czech register and Monument Catalogue,
Historic Environment Scotland, Lower Saxony, Saxony-Anhalt, the UNESCO World Heritage List,
Sweden's two, Andalusia, Catalonia, Virginia, California, Serbia, Queensland, New South
Wales, Canada and Japan. 170,214 entries captured, every one matching its endpoint count.

**Refused, with the supply recorded so the decision is visible rather than silent:**

out; historic county (118,226), located in protected area (19,857) and state of conservation

### 3b. A class census with no roots at all

Every census before this one ranks classes under roots, because the root-free form times out.
Partitioned by latitude it answers **five of twelve bands** - the sparsely populated ones - 
and even there it found four classes no root-constrained census could see. Counted globally
they hold **144,712 readable items, 111,423 of them in one class: `human settlement`
(Q486972)**, which is not a subclass of anything this project had been asking about. High
school holds 18,499, secondary school 6,753, impact crater 3,034. Captured with the 50 classes
a timed-out census batch had lost: 30 classes, **144,280 rows**.

**The seven populated bands did not answer, and that is recorded as a gap rather than as a
zero.** The readable-with-coordinates universe this instrument can see is still only partly
measured; `landmark/_readable-class-census.json` names the bands that failed.

**Two defects this pass found and fixed rather than shipped.**

*Titles.* 10,317 exact duplicate titles, one pattern throughout: two Austrian schools both
called Akademisches Gymnasium, both titled "Akademisches Gymnasium, AT" - distinct URLs,
because the item id is in the path, and a title that refused to say which town. Adding the
P131 parent to every Wikidata row was measured and was worse on both counts (titles past 65
characters 115,054 → 209,721, duplicates 10,317 → 11,405), because for the colliding pairs
the parent is also the same: two churches called St. Georg in Munich. So a locator is added
only where a name collides inside one country, chosen per row as the first field that is
unique in its group: the containing area (25,132 rows), the class (61,651), the two together
(2,993), the first stated fact (3,617), the first two facts (5,629). **436 rows remain
duplicated, and 2,368 carry nothing that separates them from another row - recorded rather
than papered over, because inventing a difference would be worse than reporting that there is
none.**

*Locale facts.* 68,216 of 186,121 destination rows carried no locale-specific fact, whole
families at a time, because the manifest computes one only for its own family-by-entity rows
and otherwise reads a field the aggregated row carries - which no Wikidata builder was
setting. The coordinate it needs has been in every capture since the first band walk. Now 704.

## What is still not measured

- **Seven of twelve latitude bands** of the root-free class census did not answer. The
  populated half of the world's readable-with-coordinates universe is unranked, and
  `human settlement` suggests what a finer partition would find.
- **36 of 299 register properties** did not answer their count batch.
- **50 classes** lost by a timed-out census batch were recovered and counted; a second batch
  of 50 in the first census remains re-countable the same way.
- Classes below 25 readable items were never counted. From the census arithmetic there are
  about 1,531 of them, holding at most ~37,000 items.
